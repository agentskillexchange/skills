# Safety and consent

## Allowed subject

Use voice evidence only when the person requesting the work is the subject, or when the subject has directly and explicitly authorised this editorial use. If consent cannot be verified, offer a non-identity-targeted style brief instead (for example: “direct, technically precise, short paragraphs”).

Consent must cover the current purpose. Permission to edit an email does not imply permission to train a reusable profile or publish a public essay.

## Evidence rules

- Voice evidence must be authored by the subject. Material written *about* the subject is not voice evidence.
- Transcripts are usable only if the subject authored the spoken words and consented to transcription and this use.
- Agent-polished text can preserve facts but must not silently become primary voice evidence.
- Hash each source and fail closed if its bytes change after the manifest was ratified.
- Keep raw paths out of generated context packs; identify excerpts by local source ID and digest.

## Draft and publication rules

Generated text is an editorial draft. The subject must review the exact artefact before it is attributed, submitted, sent, or published. Approval of an earlier version does not transfer to a revised version.

Never claim the system “is” the subject, infer undisclosed beliefs, or manufacture lived experience. For public figures or absent third parties, refuse identity imitation and offer generic, non-identifying stylistic constraints.

## Phrase leakage review

Before returning a draft, compare distinctive phrases against the context pack. Preserve reasoning motion and cadence, but rewrite any memorable phrase that is not already part of the subject's current draft or intentionally quoted with attribution.
