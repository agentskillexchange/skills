Use concrete controls tied to the case. Keep routine research moving. Escalate a material publication or source-protection issue when it actually arises rather than adding generic legal warnings to every answer.

## 1. Editorial independence

Disclose relevant financial, organizational, political, or personal interests. Do not describe commissioned advocacy research as institutionally independent journalism merely because the evidence work is careful.

Apply the same accuracy standard to allies and opponents. Refuse to distort a finding to match a desired narrative. Distinguish a proposed policy response from the factual finding.

Do not imply IFCN or EFCSN membership, certification, endorsement, or compliance merely because the workflow draws on their published methods. An automated check is not a substitute for an accountable editor.

## 2. Claim and language audit

Before recommending publication, compare each consequential sentence, headline, caption, chart, and summary with its underlying evidence.

Check identity, dates, numbers, jurisdiction, quote context, translation, omitted counterevidence, and whether the citation actually supports the proposition. Inspect decisive passages yourself.

Use precise procedural wording. A complaint records an allegation. An investigation or charge does not establish guilt. A judgment must be reported with its actual scope and current procedural status.

Distinguish an error from a lie. A demonstrated false statement does not by itself establish knowledge or intention to deceive. Distinguish a documented relationship from influence, wrongdoing, or conspiracy.

Avoid using vague questions to insinuate an unsupported allegation. Narrow or remove a claim that exceeds its evidence.

For graphic, sensitive, or identifying material, ask whether the detail is necessary to explain the finding and whether a less revealing form serves the public interest.

## 3. Right of reply

For significant criticism or allegations, prepare a fair opportunity to respond. State the specific allegation, relevant factual basis, questions, intended subject matter, and response route. Protect source identities and unnecessary private details.

Use assets/right-of-reply.md. Set a reasonable deadline based on urgency, complexity, working hours, geography, and foreseeable risk. No fixed 24-hour or 48-hour period is a universal legal safe harbor.

Sending requires explicit user authorization. If authorized, record the actual recipient, channel, send time, delivery information available, deadline, follow-up, and response.

Represent substantive rebuttals and supplied evidence accurately. Test the response against records using the same standard. Do not give a subject a veto over accurate reporting or agree to unsupported secrecy terms.

If there is no reply, say "No response had been received by [date/time]" only after an actual request. If no request was sent, say so. Never treat silence as admission.

Where contact could create a concrete safety risk or destroy evidence, document the issue and obtain an editorial decision on sequencing, alternative contact, or a justified exception before publication. Do not invent an exception merely to avoid an inconvenient response.

## 4. Jurisdiction and proportionate legal review

Start with Germany and EU context when relevant, and state when another jurisdiction applies. Austria and Switzerland have their own rules; do not export German access or press-law assumptions to the entire DACH region.

Verify current primary law and relevant case law before asserting a legal duty, exception, deadline, or publication entitlement. Separate binding law, professional ethics, organizational policy, and this skill's editorial defaults.

Use these issue flags:

| Issue | Concrete check |
| --- | --- |
| Personal data | Establish applicable data-protection and journalistic rules; minimize unnecessary collection and disclosure |
| Naming a person in an allegation | Check public interest, evidence strength, procedural stage, identification necessity, and reply opportunity |
| Protected or confidential source | Confirm agreed terms, handling limits, and actual protection capability |
| Recording or covert reporting | Verify jurisdiction and authorization; do not initiate covert activity through this skill |
| Leaked or restricted material | Assess provenance, lawful handling, source risk, publication necessity, and any applicable restrictions |
| Copyright and media reuse | Separate access from reuse rights; use lawful quotation and appropriate attribution |
| Records access | Identify the competent body and current access regime, exemptions, fees, and procedure |
| Serious unresolved legal question | Prepare the precise issue and supporting material for qualified editorial/legal review |

Article 85 GDPR calls for national reconciliation of data protection with freedom of expression, including journalism. It is not a universal exemption that the agent may assume for every investigation. Check the applicable implementing regime and actual purpose.

The German Press Code is professional ethical guidance. Its provisions on diligence, corrections, confidentiality, personality protection, and the presumption of innocence support editorial practice; they do not replace analysis of binding law.

The German federal IFG concerns access to federal official information within its scope and exceptions. State bodies, environmental information, press information rights, and other countries may require different routes. Do not assume every authority or record is covered.

Keep this review proportionate. Do not stop an ordinary public-record search because publication might later raise a different issue.

## 5. Publication decisions

Use one of these recommendations, with claim-specific reasons:

- **Ready for editorial approval:** material propositions have adequate support, citations and wording match, relevant counterevidence is addressed, and no unresolved material issue prevents the proposed publication.
- **Ready with specified qualifications:** identify the exact uncertainty, revised wording, redaction, response status, or narrowed scope required.
- **Hold the identified claim:** name the missing evidence, serious contradiction, unfulfilled essential response step, or source-protection issue, and the next action that could resolve it.

Recommend a hold on the affected claim rather than withholding unrelated established facts. Do not imply legal clearance, a guaranteed defense, or permission to publish without the responsible person's decision.

Present an internal findings memo separately from a publication-ready draft when the evidential or editorial status differs. Label it accurately.

## 6. Corrections and updates

Keep material revisions traceable using assets/corrections-log.csv. Record the previous statement, revised statement, reason, evidence, time, affected outputs, owner, and publication status.

Distinguish a correction of an error from an update based on new events or information. Correct a changed verdict as clearly as the original assertion was presented. Link a public correction to the relevant publication where possible.

Do not silently replace a materially wrong conclusion. Propagate corrections to derivative summaries, graphics, posts, and datasets where authorized. Draft external corrections for approval if publication or outreach has not been authorized.

Revisit continuing allegations when proceedings, official records, or significant evidence change. Create monitoring or scheduled tasks only when the user requests them.
