## 1. Claim selection and decomposition

Select questions by public importance, potential harm, relevance, and available evidence. Record why a low-reach rumor warrants investigation before amplifying it. Avoid choosing only claims that support a client's position.

Turn a sentence into atomic propositions. For example, the fictional claim "Harbor City secretly paid Northbridge Ltd twice the agreed price because the mayor owns it" contains at least these checks:

- The identity of the purchasing body and exact legal entity.
- The contract, original price, modifications, and payments.
- Whether the relevant information was disclosed and where.
- Ownership or control at the relevant date.
- Any causal link between that relationship and the purchasing decision.
- The additional allegation of intent or concealment.

Do not let proof of a payment substitute for proof of influence or criminal intent. Establish what a speaker actually said separately from whether it was accurate.

Record definitions before comparing quantities: approved budget versus expenditure, contract ceiling versus paid amount, beneficiary versus contractor, people versus cases, annual versus cumulative, alleged versus adjudicated. Distinguish the claim as made at the time from a claim about the present.

## 2. Triage and research planning

Use SIFT as a discovery discipline: pause to define the task, investigate the source, find stronger coverage, and return claims to their original context. Use lateral reading to assess a publisher through independent sources rather than its self-description.

Turn the research question into an evidence plan:

| Question | Most useful record | Likely holder | Relevant period | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What was formally decided? | Signed decision, minutes, roll-call record | Responsible institution | Decision date | Inspect original and amendments |
| What actually happened? | Transaction, measurement, contemporaneous record, direct witness | Operator, regulator, witness | Event interval | Cross-check independent observations |
| Who controlled the entity? | Ownership records and governing documents | Relevant register or entity | Effective dates | Resolve legal identifiers |
| Was a public assertion accurate? | Full statement plus underlying data | Speaker and data producer | Claim date | Reproduce comparison |

Search exact phrases and distinctive fragments, then concepts and synonyms. Use date windows, local languages, formal entity names, former names, and institutional terminology. Expand beyond a single search engine or platform when coverage is material.

Distinguish discovery priority from final evidential weight. A low-quality source can supply a useful lead; it does not become proof because the lead was productive.

## 3. Competing hypotheses

For disputed explanations, maintain two or more plausible hypotheses where the evidence warrants them. Include "insufficient evidence to distinguish" rather than forcing a winner.

Use a simplified analysis-of-competing-hypotheses matrix:

- Describe the observation independently of the preferred explanation.
- Mark whether it supports, conflicts with, or fails to discriminate between each hypothesis.
- Record the source-dependency group and observation quality.
- Identify the observation that would most strongly separate the remaining hypotheses.
- Revisit the matrix after new evidence.

Give more attention to strong contradictions than to many weak compatible clues. Do not total matrix symbols as if they were calibrated probabilities. A fact compatible with every hypothesis has little power to distinguish them.

Keep assumptions explicit: completeness of a register, accuracy of a timestamp, absence of undisclosed contracts, identity of similarly named entities. Test sensitivity to assumptions that control the result.

Do not infer intent from outcome alone. Investigate capability, opportunity, decisions, communications, incentives, and alternative explanations before making an intentionality claim. Never present an untestable motive narrative as established fact.

## 4. Source and evidence assessment

Record two separate assessments.

**Source assessment:** identify authorship or institutional responsibility, first-hand access, relevant expertise, interests, prior reliability when documented, anonymity conditions, and dependencies on others.

**Evidence-item assessment:** determine what this particular item shows, whether it is complete, how it was obtained, whether it can be authenticated, whether its dates fit, and what alternative interpretations remain.

Use descriptive reasons rather than an averaged credibility score. Prestige cannot cure an irrelevant item. An interested source can provide an authentic document; an authentic document can contain a mistaken statement.

Prefer records appropriate to the proposition:

| Material | What it can establish | What requires separate verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Official minutes or division list | What the institution recorded | Completeness, later correction, conduct outside the record |
| Corporate filing | Recorded legal details and assertions at the filing date | Accuracy of declarations, beneficial control, current status |
| Court complaint or indictment | That an allegation was formally made | The allegation's truth or guilt |
| Judgment or order | The court's findings or operative decision within scope | Finality, appeals, issues not decided |
| Eyewitness account | The witness's account of what they could observe | Visibility, memory, incentives, inference, corroboration |
| Video or photograph | Visible or audible content of the available recording | Origin, context, capture time/place, unseen events |
| Dataset | Values and definitions in that version | Coverage, collection error, comparability, causal interpretation |
| Anonymous tip or leak | A research lead and possibly inspectable evidence | Identity, access, authenticity, completeness, motive |

Use AI for organizing, extracting, translating, or proposing checks. Verify decisive output against the underlying material. Do not cite the model as factual corroboration.

## 5. Corroboration and negative evidence

Assign an origin group to each evidence stream. Track whether articles depend on a press release, wire report, shared witness, report, dataset, or copied post. Different URLs and different publishers do not establish independence.

Require independent relevance as well as independence. Two people repeating the same rumor do not verify an event. Two measurements generated by the same faulty instrument may share the decisive failure.

State the scope of a sole-source finding. A primary vote record can settle how a named member's vote was recorded. A company's statement alone cannot settle whether its conduct complied with that statement.

Treat missing evidence carefully. Before using absence as evidence, establish:

1. The system would normally record the event if it occurred.
2. The relevant institution, period, geography, identifiers, and record classes are covered.
3. Publication delay, retention, access restrictions, indexing, and exemptions are understood.
4. Plausible alternative repositories and names were checked.

Use "No matching award was located in the specified register and date range" when that is what was established. Do not upgrade it to "No award exists" without a defensible completeness basis.

A deleted post can establish that a previously captured item is no longer available through the checked route. It does not establish why it disappeared.

## 6. Interviews and source contact

Prepare an interview plan around what the source directly knows and what records could corroborate it. Ask open questions before presenting a theory. Separate recollection, hearsay, expertise, and inference.

Agree attribution and recording terms before proceeding. Do not assume "background" and "off the record" have universally agreed meanings. Do not promise protection the actual tools and storage cannot provide.

For anonymous sources, document internally why anonymity is necessary, how access to the information was assessed, and what independent corroboration exists. Keep the identity key outside general case files and published outputs.

Do not pressure vulnerable people, pay for allegations, impersonate an official, or manufacture a pretext. Draft contact materials first. Send them only when explicitly authorized and through an appropriate available channel.

## 7. Confidence, verdicts, and publication decisions

Apply the verdict vocabulary in SKILL.md to the smallest useful claim. Explain any aggregate verdict. Use "mixed" only when different components can actually be separated.

Use these confidence defaults:

- **High:** decisive evidence was inspected; provenance, definitions, and relevant alternatives were checked; no material unresolved contradiction changes the stated finding.
- **Medium:** the finding is supported but a meaningful gap, dependency, or interpretation remains; narrow the wording to fit.
- **Low:** evidence is indirect, weak, incomplete, conflicting, or dependent; identify the missing test.

Confidence concerns the assessment as stated. It can be high that a claim is presently unsubstantiated while the claim's underlying truth remains unknown. Make that distinction explicit when relevant.

Do not equate confidence with permission to name a person, publish a sensitive location, or allege a crime. Accuracy, necessity, source protection, and editorial responsibility are separate questions.

## 8. Independent review and stopping rules

Give a reviewer the neutral question, actual artifacts, and source register. Ask for citation mismatches, overlooked context, contradictions, dependence between sources, alternative explanations, arithmetic errors, and overstatement.

Avoid passing a desired conclusion to the reviewer. Preserve disagreements and record how they were resolved. Have an accountable human editor assess serious allegations and protected-source issues before publication.

Complete the work when either:

- The defined claims have adequate evidence and material contrary evidence has been addressed.
- The remaining uncertainty has been located precisely and the next discriminating evidence is unavailable or outside authorization.

Do not treat a deadline as evidence. Deliver the current finding with an as-of date, unresolved issue, and practical next action. Do not schedule continued monitoring unless requested.
