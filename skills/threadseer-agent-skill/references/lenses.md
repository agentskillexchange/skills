# Analytical lenses

Select only the lenses that help answer the request. A profile supplies defaults, but source content determines whether a lens has anything meaningful to report.

## Executive lens

Answer:

- What happened?
- What now matters most?
- Why does it matter at this moment?
- What decision pressure or opportunity exists?

Avoid chronological play-by-play. Surface the consequential state change.

## Decision lens

Extract:

- settled decisions and their conditions;
- proposed but unsettled choices;
- rejected and superseded options when they explain the tradeoff;
- decision owners or authorities when explicit;
- decisions still required.

Keep participant decisions separate from analyst recommendations.

## Execution lens

Identify actions, owners, deadlines, dependencies, blockers, risks, and the next physical or communicative step. Rank using urgency, impact, feasibility, dependency order, reversibility, and learning value.

Use `Unassigned` when no owner is stated. A task with no authority or prerequisite should not be ranked as immediately executable.

## Reasoning lens

Reconstruct the argument:

- goal or decision criterion;
- evidence offered;
- assumptions;
- alternatives;
- tradeoffs;
- conclusion;
- missing evidence that could change the conclusion.

Distinguish a weak argument from a poorly transcribed one.

## Alignment lens

Find:

- explicit disagreement;
- apparent agreement using different definitions;
- incompatible objectives or time horizons;
- ambiguity about ownership or authority;
- stakeholders who are discussed but absent;
- decisions that participants may interpret differently.

Do not force consensus. State the smallest clarification that would resolve each ambiguity.

## Risk lens

Consider only relevant categories:

- strategic;
- operational;
- technical;
- financial;
- legal or compliance;
- reputational;
- coordination;
- dependency;
- epistemic or evidence quality;
- privacy and disclosure.

For each material risk, state evidence, likelihood qualitatively, consequence, trigger, and mitigation or test. Do not fabricate numerical probabilities.

## Learning lens

Extract:

- new information;
- beliefs strengthened, weakened, or corrected;
- reusable methods and principles;
- failed approaches and why they failed;
- questions whose answers would create the most learning.

Separate durable learning from meeting-specific detail.

## Opportunity lens

Classify grounded opportunities as:

- **Quick win:** cheap, reversible, and immediately useful.
- **Leverage point:** modest intervention with disproportionate downstream benefit.
- **Small moonshot:** ambitious but testable without existential commitment.
- **Moonshot:** potentially transformative and materially uncertain.

For every nontrivial opportunity, provide the mechanism, enabling evidence, principal uncertainty, and cheapest informative test.

## Nonlinear-impact lens

Activate only when the material supports one or more mechanisms:

- compounding learning or data advantage;
- coordination breakthrough;
- platform or ecosystem effects;
- positive feedback loop;
- major bottleneck removal;
- large capability or cost discontinuity;
- institutional replication at scale.

Describe the causal mechanism and necessary conditions. Distinguish `interesting`, `high impact`, and `system-changing`; they are not synonyms. If the evidence supports no nonlinear mechanism, omit the section or state that none was identified.

## Institutional-memory lens

Preserve only what a future participant needs to resume accurately:

- current state;
- decisions and rationale;
- active commitments and custody;
- unresolved questions;
- assumptions and constraints;
- rejected paths worth not repeating;
- canonical artefacts or source pointers;
- next review trigger.

Do not archive conversational filler or the entire transcript by default. Durable storage remains a separately authorized action.

## Communication lens

Adapt the same evidence to the audience:

- lead with shared context and outcomes;
- include only actionable detail;
- distinguish information, request, and decision;
- make the requested response and deadline explicit;
- exclude private reasoning from a shareable report.

A follow-up draft must not imply it has been sent.

## Adversarial lens

Stress-test the emerging synthesis:

- What is the strongest alternative interpretation?
- What evidence contradicts the leading recommendation?
- Which dependency or authority assumption is hidden?
- What would make the plan fail despite competent execution?
- Which cheap test could falsify the key assumption?
- Is the proposed action reversible, and what is the cost of waiting?

Use this lens to improve the recommendation, not to generate performative objections.

## Comparative lens

For multiple conversations or a prior register, report:

- new, unchanged, superseded, reversed, completed, and stalled items;
- changes in confidence or rationale;
- recurring unresolved themes;
- newly introduced risks and dependencies;
- commitments that lack evidence of completion.

Do not infer completion from absence in the later conversation.
